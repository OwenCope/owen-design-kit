# Site gallery

Real sites, captured 2026-10-11 in `shots/<site>/`: `hero.jpg`, `section-01.jpg` onwards (each a 900 px scroll step), and `phone-hero.jpg`. `shots/index.json` lists the files and URLs. Some captures include cookie banners, and on Arc the product images failed to load; ignore those.

How to use it: open the shots, read the note, then ask what the equivalent decision is for your product. If your page could be described as "their page with our logo", start again. Measured values for Linear, Raycast, Stripe, Notion and Duolingo are in `design-directions/research/`.

## The sites

**apple-airpods** (apple.com/airpods-pro): the hero is one photograph, a person in silhouette with the product as a small white dot at their ear. The product doesn't need to be big when the picture explains the feeling. Price and Buy sit quietly in the corner. Don't copy: the photography budget. Copy: let one picture carry the headline.

**linear-method** (linear.app/method): a long-form essay page, not a product page. One serif headline on black ("Practices for building") and a short paragraph. It sells the product by showing how the company thinks. Copy: a page about your beliefs can sell better than a feature list, if you really have them.

**raycast-store** (raycast.com/store): the hero is a grid of real extension icons in glass tiles, fading at the edges, with a search field under the title. It shows the size of the catalogue without a number. Copy: show the real things, many of them, and let the eye fill in "lots".

**arc** (arc.net): playful and loud on purpose: a saturated blue band with wavy edges and a quote from a review as the headline. Breaks most rules, and works because the browser itself was playful. Don't copy unless the product has that personality.

**things** (culturedcode.com/things): almost nothing. The app icon, the name, two lines, a video link, lots of soft grey space. It works because the icon is beautiful and the app is famous for calm. Copy: if your icon is strong, let it be the hero. Don't copy: the emptiness, if your product needs explaining.

**vercel** (vercel.com): monochrome, the brand triangle as the only picture, a two-word headline left, three short lines right, customer logos as a strip. Confidence through restraint. Copy: one graphic element, no colour, real customer names.

**figma** (figma.com): a collage of real things made in Figma (posters, a type specimen, app screens) stacked in the centre, headline left, button right. The product is shown through its output. Copy: for a creation tool, show what people made with it, not the tool.

**framer** (framer.com): the real editor at work in the hero, dark, with an AI panel actively building a page. It shows the product doing the job, mid-task. Copy: a hero that shows the moment of use beats a screenshot of the empty app.

**superhuman** (superhuman.com): split hero, dark text side and a real inbox with an AI draft appearing in the reply. Below, a three-row list of products. Copy: show the one feature that saves time, inside the real UI.

**notion-calendar** (notion.com/product/calendar): the app icon floats among small coloured spot icons, the headline is two words ("It's time."), and the real calendar sits on a MacBook below. Copy: a short, playful headline earns its place when the product shot underneath does the explaining.

**stripe-payments** (stripe.com/payments): the hero shows a real, working checkout form next to the headline. For a payments product, the checkout is the product. Copy: put the actual moment of use in the hero.

**rauno** (rauno.me): a portfolio that is one giant headline with a yellow circle and a custom crosshair cursor. Pure personality, every other element held back. Copy only for personal sites: one idea, all the way.

**teenage-engineering** (teenage.engineering): black-and-white photography of their own store, thin line icons as navigation, tiny type. Feels like a design studio, which it is. Don't copy for products that need clarity.

**posthog** (posthog.com): a desktop operating system as a website: windows, icons down both sides, a hedgehog mascot, jokes in the copy ("Legally-required cookie banner"). Dense and fun because its audience (developers) likes exploring. Copy: personality in the writing. Don't copy: the OS metaphor.

## What they share

- The product, or what it produces, is visible in the first screen on nearly every one.
- One idea per hero. None of them has three competing pictures.
- The headline is short, often two to six words.
- Colour is used in one place, if at all.
- The voice matches who the audience is, not what "a landing page" sounds like.
