These are reasoning to learn from, not ideas to reuse.
Copying a product idea from here fails review.
Each brief ends with the generic version a model would make by default, so you can recognise it in your own work.

## Tide

1. The one thing: Shows your next event and counts it down where you already look.
2. The proof: At 9:58 the notch reads Design review 2:00, and at 9:58 it chimes once while the event card slides down from the menu bar.
3. The hero frame at 1440 x 900: A MacBook at 1100 px wide, centred, top edge 90 px from the viewport top. The notch shows Design review 4:58 in 15 pt, readable at full width. Below it a single event card, 420 px wide, with a 34 pt title, a 15 pt time row, and a Join button. Calm light wallpaper. Nothing else competes.
4. What moves: Scrolling moves time forward. The visitor scrolls a 250 vh section and the notch countdown falls from 5:00 to 0:00. At the end the chime fires as a soft pulse and the real event card opens. The motion ends on the real product.
5. Three concepts, one sentence each:
   - Scroll is time passing and the notch countdown burns down to the chime.
   - The pointer drags a meeting block across a day strip and the notch updates live.
   - A centred headline with a small countdown chip under it that ticks on load (the safe default).
6. The pick and why: Pick the first. It rejects the safe default because a ticking chip shows a feature while scrolling through time proves the verb.
7. What is cut:
   - Feature cards, because the countdown already says what Tide does.
   - A logos row, because a new app has no logos and invented ones read as false.
   - A pricing table, because one promise needs one button, not three tiers.
   - An FAQ, because two sentences of real copy answer the only question.
   - A footer sitemap, because a single page needs a single line of small text.
8. Voice:
   - Headline: Never miss the next thing.
   - Sub line: Your next event lives in the notch, with a chime two minutes before.
   - Feature line: Countdown, chime, Join. That is the whole app.
9. Done means:
   - Screenshot at 1440 x 900 shows the notch text readable without zoom.
   - Screenshot at 390 px wide shows the event card full width with the Join button reachable.
   - Screenshot with Reduce Motion on shows the final event card with the countdown at 0:00.
10. The generic version:
    - Centred headline with a gradient word and a ticking chip below, which is weaker because it decorates time instead of letting the visitor spend it.
    - A fake browser frame with letter-tile calendar icons, which is weaker because drawn UI never beats the real menu bar text.
    - A dark demo box with pill chips for features, which is weaker because chips list promises while the countdown proves one.
    - Three feature cards for countdown, chime and sync, which is weaker because three equal cards hide the one thing.
    - A Questions, answered FAQ with five invented questions, which is weaker because it answers doubts nobody voiced on a one-promise page.
    - The same closing download band as every other page, which is weaker because repetition tells the visitor this product is interchangeable.

## Stash

1. The one thing: Keeps everything you copy one click away in the menu bar.
2. The proof: The visitor selects a real sentence on the page and a dated slip of that exact text stacks onto a shelf at the top edge within a second.
3. The hero frame at 1440 x 900: A MacBook at 1100 px wide, centred, menu bar fully visible. The Stash icon sits third from the right with a small 3 badge. An open panel, 360 px wide and 480 px tall, hangs from the icon showing five real clips with 13 pt text and 12 pt timestamps. The page behind is a short paragraph worth selecting.
4. What moves: Selecting text is the trigger. Each selection pins a slip where it was and flies a copy into the menu bar icon, which bounces once. Scrolling back walks the shelf newest first. It ends on the real open panel.
5. Three concepts, one sentence each:
   - Selected words peel off the page and fly into the menu bar icon, then fan out as a deck on scroll.
   - A timeline of the day where each copy lands on the hour it happened.
   - A hero with a search field over a static list of grey placeholder clips (the safe default).
6. The pick and why: Pick the first. It rejects the safe default because typing in a fake search box demonstrates nothing while keeping a real selection proves the verb.
7. What is cut:
   - A how it works strip with three numbered steps, because the first selection teaches it faster.
   - Stock testimonial quotes, because invented praise damages trust in a tool that holds private text.
   - A feature grid with sync, search and dark mode icons, because the panel already shows search on real text.
   - A video modal, because the page itself is the demo and a play button hides it.
   - A newsletter signup, because a clipboard tool asking for email before proving itself reads as needy.
8. Voice:
   - Headline: Copy once. Keep it.
   - Sub line: Every copy waits in the menu bar, newest on top.
   - Feature line: Click a clip to paste it back exactly as it was.
9. Done means:
   - Screenshot after selecting text shows a slip with the exact selected words and a date.
   - Screenshot at 1440 x 900 shows all five panel rows readable with no truncation past two lines.
   - Screenshot with Reduce Motion on shows the full panel open with the newest clip on top.
10. The generic version:
    - Centred headline with a fake Mac and letter-tile clip icons, which is weaker because tiles hide the real text the product exists to keep.
    - A dark demo box with chips for text, links and images, which is weaker because chips categorise instead of showing a single real copy landing.
    - Three feature cards for history, search and sync, which is weaker because equal cards flatten keeping into one of three equals.
    - A Questions, answered FAQ about encryption and limits, which is weaker because it spends the page on worries before the visitor has copied anything.
    - The same closing band with two buttons, which is weaker because a second call to action splits the one habit this page must build.

## Skyline

1. The one thing: Tells the next hour of sky from the menu bar.
2. The proof: Dragging across a thin hour strip changes the section light from pale morning to grey rain at 3 pm, and the street scene puddles match.
3. The hero frame at 1440 x 900: A MacBook at 1000 px wide, centred, top edge 110 px down. The menu bar shows a cloud symbol with 61° and 3 pm rain in 13 pt. Under the headline a 720 px hour strip runs 2 pm to 6 pm with rain arriving at the third tick. One small street photo, 640 px wide, sits beside it with wet pavement.
4. What moves: The visitor drags across the hour strip. Light, shadow and the street photo shift to that hour. Lifting the pointer settles on now. It ends on the real menu bar forecast.
5. Three concepts, one sentence each:
   - The page light itself is the forecast and dragging the strip moves the sun and rain across it.
   - Scrolling walks a full day from sunrise to night behind the product window.
   - A blue gradient hero with a big 72° numeral and small day cards below (the safe default).
6. The pick and why: Pick the first. It rejects the safe default because a big numeral states weather while moving light lets the visitor feel the hour change.
7. What is cut:
   - A seven-day forecast grid, because this product owns the next hour, not the week.
   - Animated clouds drifting on load, because decoration moves without the visitor and says nothing.
   - A cities picker with ten preset cities, because one real street proves more than ten names.
   - A feature comparison table against other weather apps, because comparison invites doubt on a first visit.
   - Social share buttons, because nobody shares a forecast before trusting it once.
8. Voice:
   - Headline: The next hour, out there.
   - Sub line: Skyline watches the sky so you can pick the right moment to leave.
   - Feature line: Drag the strip. The light tells you when the rain starts.
9. Done means:
   - Screenshot at 3 pm tick shows grey light and the menu bar reading rain.
   - Screenshot at 390 px wide shows the hour strip scrollable with the current hour centred.
   - Screenshot with Reduce Motion on shows the strip with the now hour selected and no mid-drag state.
10. The generic version:
    - Centred headline over a blue gradient with a giant temperature numeral, which is weaker because a numeral could belong to any weather app.
    - A fake phone with letter-tile day icons, which is weaker because drawn icons replace the one real proof, which is light.
    - A dark demo box with chips for rain, wind and UV, which is weaker because chips flatten the hour into three facts of equal weight.
    - Three feature cards for radar, alerts and widgets, which is weaker because cards describe breadth while the product wins on one narrow hour.
    - A Questions, answered FAQ about data sources, which is weaker because sourcing trivia earns no trust before the visitor drags the strip.
    - The same closing band as the other pages, which is weaker because identical endings teach the visitor the products are identical.

## Frame

1. The one thing: Captures, marks up and shares the screen in one gesture.
2. The proof: The visitor drags a rectangle over the page itself, edge rulers read 840 x 520, and on release the real editor opens with that exact shot inside.
3. The hero frame at 1440 x 900: A full-bleed page section dimmed to 40 percent with a clear 840 x 520 px capture rectangle centred. Rulers on two edges read the pixel size in 12 pt. Inside the rectangle the page stays sharp. A small toolbar, 320 px wide, floats on the rectangle edge with arrow, text and share symbols.
4. What moves: The pointer drag is the motion. Press dims the page, drag resizes the frame with live ruler numbers, release opens the real editor with the shot placed. It ends on the real product.
5. Concepts: none — the drag-to-frame gesture is the brief; the open question is only its trigger tolerance, settled in the prototype.
6. The pick and why: No concepts — the drag itself is the idea; the only open question is trigger tolerance, settled in the prototype.
7. What is cut:
   - A formats row listing PNG, JPG and GIF badges, because formats are settings, not reasons to try.
   - A keyboard shortcut table, because shortcuts matter on day ten, not on first sight.
   - A testimonial carousel, because borrowed praise cannot prove a gesture the visitor just performed.
   - A changelog block, because version history answers a question a new visitor never asked.
   - A second product screenshot gallery, because the captured shot already is the gallery.
8. Voice:
   - Headline: Drag. Done. Sent.
   - Sub line: Frame takes the shot, lets you mark it, and hands you the link.
   - Feature line: One drag from screen to shared link, rulers included.
9. Done means:
   - Screenshot mid-drag shows dimmed page, sharp rectangle and ruler numbers all readable.
   - Screenshot after release shows the editor with the captured region matching the dragged size.
   - Screenshot at 390 px wide shows the toolbar reachable with no ruler text clipped.
10. The generic version:
    - Centred headline with three screenshots in a row for capture, annotate and share, which is weaker because three pictures narrate while one gesture proves.
    - A fake Mac with letter-tile toolbar icons, which is weaker because drawn tools hide the real editor the visitor must trust.
    - A dark demo box with chips for blur, arrow and text, which is weaker because chips name tools without letting the hand use one.
    - Three feature cards with stock icons, which is weaker because cards split one gesture into three products.
    - A Questions, answered FAQ about storage and pricing, which is weaker because paperwork before the first drag kills momentum.
    - The same closing download band, which is weaker because ending every product identically erases the one gesture just learned.

## What all four have in common

- Start from the verb: each moment performs what the product does, and removing the verb breaks the concept.
- The product is the largest readable thing: real menu bar text, real clips, real light, real editor, sized to read at full width.
- The motion is the product working, caused by the visitor: scroll spends time, selection keeps text, drag moves weather, drag frames the screen.
- Cut more than you add: every page leaves out cards, FAQs, logos, tables and closings that do not prove the verb.
- Copy has a voice: short plain lines that sound like the tool, never interchangeable feature sentences.
- Done is defined before building: three screenshot checks per brief, including a small screen and Reduce Motion.
